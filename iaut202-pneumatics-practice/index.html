IAUT202 — Pneumatic cascade practice (9 sequences)

Nine sequences to group and turn into an electrical cascade: three-cylinder sequences, one with a 3 s delay and one with four cylinders. Try each one on paper, then open the memo for the groups, relay set and reset conditions, and the solenoid table.

Sequence 1 · 3 groups

A+ A- B+ B-

Cylinders A, B, each with end sensors X0 (retracted) and X1 (extended) and a double-solenoid 5/2 valve (A+ = Y1, A- = Y2, B+ = Y3, B- = Y4, and so on). All cylinders are retracted at rest. Sequence: A+ A- B+ B-. (a) Divide it into groups. (b) Give the set and reset condition of each group relay. (c) Give the condition that energises each solenoid. (d) State the sensors pressed at rest.

Show memo

(a) Groups. A new group starts as soon as a cylinder letter would repeat: [A+] | [A- B+] | [B-]. That is 3 groups, so 3 group relays K1 to K3.

(b) Relays. Each relay latches itself (hold contact) and is switched off by the next group's relay, so only one group is live at a time.

RelaySet byReset by (NC contact in series)
K1Start · B0 (cycle finished, everything home)K2
K2A1 (confirms A+, the last move of group 1)K3
K3B1 (confirms B+, the last move of group 2)K1

(c) Solenoids. The first move of a group is switched by the group relay alone. Every other move needs the group relay AND the sensor that confirms the move before it.

StepMoveSolenoidEnergised by
1A+Y1K1
2A-Y2K2
3B+Y3K2 · A0
4B-Y4K3

(d) Checks. At rest all cylinders are retracted, so A0, B0 are pressed (that includes B0 for Start). Two opposite solenoids of one cylinder (e.g. Y1 and Y2) are never on together because the groups switch them at different times. Order of moves from the simulation: A+ A- B+ B-.

Every memo is built by one rule: a new group starts when a cylinder letter would repeat; the first move of a group is switched by the group relay, every other move by the group relay AND the sensor of the move before it; group relay Kn+1 is set by the sensor that confirms the last move of group n. Each sequence was checked with a small time-step simulation of this rule (delays not simulated). The rule follows the lecturer's cascade examples as recorded in the project notes; check any variation your class used. Every sequence here has three or more groups; a sequence with only two groups needs a different arrangement (one relay, not one per group) that is not covered on this page.
