How to use these papers

  1. Sit each paper in one go, timed: 90 minutes. Calculator, the formula sheet and tables only (the November 2026 formula sheet & tables PDF from Moodle — pages 20–25 cover everything for Test 2). No notes.
  2. Write Part 1 on paper exactly as you would in the test: define the random variable, write the formula, substitute, answer to 3 dp. For hypothesis tests write all six steps.
  3. Do Part 2 by noting your letters (a–e) on paper first. Then click through here to check them.
  4. Mark Part 1 against the memo using the [mark] allocations. Method marks count — give yourself the marks for correct set-up even if the arithmetic slipped.
Timing guide: about 1.8 minutes per mark. Part 1 (30 marks) ≈ 55 minutes; Part 2 (20 MCQs) ≈ 30 minutes; 5 minutes to check. If an MCQ takes more than 2 minutes, skip it and come back.
Hypothesis-test marking: the lecturer says the six steps are "six marks" — roughly one per step. The mocks give 8 marks per test so the working can be marked in finer detail (2 for the hypotheses, 2 for the test statistic); on the real paper expect ≈ 1 mark per step.

Mock APart 1 — Long Questions [30]

Question 1 [7]

The number of insurance claims, X, that a small broker receives per day has the following probability distribution:

x01234
P(X = x)0.100.25k0.200.05

(i) Determine the value of k. [1]
(ii) Determine P(1 < X ≤ 3). [1]
(iii) Calculate the expected number of claims per day. [2]
(iv) Calculate the standard deviation of the number of claims per day. [3]

Memo — Question 1
(i) 0.10 + 0.25 + k + 0.20 + 0.05 = 1 ⇒ k = 0.40 ✓
(ii) P(1 < X ≤ 3) = P(2) + P(3) = 0.40 + 0.20 = 0.600 ✓
(iii) E(X) = 0(0.10) + 1(0.25) + 2(0.40) + 3(0.20) + 4(0.05) ✓ = 1.850 ✓
(iv) E(X²) = 0 + 0.25 + 1.60 + 1.80 + 0.80 = 4.45 ✓   V(X) = 4.45 − 1.85² = 1.0275 ✓   σ = √1.0275 = 1.014 ✓

Question 2 [7]

A clothing retailer finds that 20% of customers who enter the store make a purchase. Customers make their decisions independently. Twelve customers enter the store.

(i) Define the random variable and state its distribution, with parameters. [2]
(ii) What is the probability that exactly three customers make a purchase? [2]
(iii) What is the probability that at least two customers make a purchase? [3]

Memo — Question 2
(i) X = the number of customers (out of 12) who make a purchase ✓.   X ~ BI(12, 0.2) ✓
(ii) P(X = 3) = ₁₂C₃(0.2)³(0.8)⁹ ✓ = 220 × 0.008 × 0.134218 = 0.236 ✓
(iii) P(X ≥ 2) = 1 − [P(X = 0) + P(X = 1)] ✓ = 1 − [0.0687 + 0.2062] ✓ = 0.725 ✓

Question 3 [8]

The volume of cooldrink in 500 ml bottles filled by a machine is normally distributed with mean 500 ml and variance 144 ml².

(i) What proportion of bottles contain less than 485 ml? [2]
(ii) What is the probability that a bottle contains between 490 ml and 520 ml? [2]
(iii) Above what volume are the fullest 5% of bottles? [2]
(iv) A quality inspector takes a random sample of 16 bottles. What is the probability that the sample mean volume exceeds 505 ml? [2]

Memo — Question 3

X ~ N(500, 144) ⇒ σ = 12.

(i) P(X < 485) = P(Z < (485 − 500)/12) = P(Z < −1.25) ✓ = 1 − 0.8944 = 0.106 ✓
(ii) P(490 < X < 520) = P(−0.83 < Z < 1.67) ✓ = 0.9525 − (1 − 0.7967) = 0.749 ✓
(iii) P(X > k) = 0.05 ⇒ P(Z < z) = 0.95 ⇒ z = 1.645 ✓ ⇒ k = 500 + 1.645(12) = 519.740 ml ✓ (z = 1.64 ⇒ 519.680, also accepted)
(iv) X̄ ~ N(500, 144/16) ⇒ σx̄ = 12/4 = 3. P(X̄ > 505) = P(Z > 1.67) ✓ = 1 − 0.9525 = 0.048 ✓

Question 4 [8]

A company compares two training programmes. Ten employees who completed Programme A had a mean productivity score of 52.3 with a standard deviation of 4.1. Twelve employees who completed Programme B had a mean score of 47.6 with a standard deviation of 5.0. Assume the scores are normally distributed with equal population variances. Test at the 5% level of significance whether Programme A produces a higher mean score than Programme B.

Memo — Question 4
1. H₀: μA = μB    H₁: μA > μB ✓✓
2. α = 0.05 ✓
3. sp = √[(9 × 4.1² + 11 × 5.0²)/20] = √21.3145 = 4.617 ✓
    tts = (52.3 − 47.6)/(4.617 √(1/10 + 1/12)) = 4.7/1.977 = 2.378 ✓
4. tcrit = t20; 0.95 = 1.725 ✓
5. Reject H₀ if tts > 1.725. Since 2.378 > 1.725, reject H₀ ✓
6. At the 5% level of significance there is sufficient evidence to conclude that Programme A produces a higher mean productivity score than Programme B ✓
Why pooled t: two independent groups, both small (10 and 12), normal, equal variances stated. df = 10 + 12 − 2 = 20.

For questions 1 and 2: a call centre receives complaints at an average rate of 4.5 per hour, following a Poisson distribution.

Question 1
The probability of exactly 3 complaints in an hour is:
  • a) 0.333
  • b) 0.169
  • c) 0.174
  • d) 0.011
  • e) None of the above
P(X = 3) = e−4.5(4.5)³/3! = 0.1687.
Question 2
The probability of at least 2 complaints in a 30-minute period is:
  • a) 0.939
  • b) 0.343
  • c) 0.657
  • d) 0.237
  • e) None of the above
Rescale: λ = 4.5/2 = 2.25. P(Y ≥ 2) = 1 − [P(0) + P(1)] = 1 − (0.1054 + 0.2371) = 0.6575. (a) is what you get with λ = 4.5.
Question 3
2% of items from a production line are defective. X is the number of defective items in a box of 50. X follows a:
  • a) Binomial distribution
  • b) Poisson distribution
  • c) Normal distribution
  • d) t-distribution
  • e) None of the above
Fixed n = 50 trials, two outcomes, constant p = 0.02, independent ⇒ binomial.
Question 4
X ~ N(60, 25). According to the empirical rule, approximately 95% of values lie between:
  • a) 35 and 85
  • b) 55 and 65
  • c) 10 and 110
  • d) 50 and 70
  • e) None of the above
σ = √25 = 5. μ ± 2σ = 60 ± 10 ⇒ (50 ; 70).
Question 5
P(Z < 1.73) equals:
  • a) 0.0418
  • b) 0.9582
  • c) 0.4582
  • d) 0.9591
  • e) None of the above
Read directly: row 1.7, column 0.03 ⇒ 0.9582.
Question 6
P(−1.5 < Z < 0.5) equals:
  • a) 0.2417
  • b) 0.3753
  • c) 0.6247
  • d) 1.6247
  • e) None of the above
P(Z < 0.5) − P(Z < −1.5) = 0.6915 − (1 − 0.9332) = 0.6247.
Question 7
For a continuous random variable X, which statement is true?
  • a) P(X < 5) = P(X ≤ 5)
  • b) P(X = 5) = f(5)
  • c) f(x) can be greater than 1 only if X is discrete
  • d) The probabilities of all outcomes sum to more than 1
  • e) None of the above
P(X = 5) = 0 for a continuous variable, so including or excluding the endpoint changes nothing.
Question 8
The Central Limit Theorem is usually considered to apply when:
  • a) n ≤ 5
  • b) n ≥ 10
  • c) σ is known
  • d) n ≥ 30
  • e) None of the above
For a non-normal population, x̄ is approximately normal for sufficiently large n, usually n ≥ 30.
Question 9
A population has mean 80 and standard deviation 20. For samples of size 100, the standard error of the mean is:
  • a) 0.2
  • b) 2
  • c) 4
  • d) 20
  • e) None of the above
σ/√n = 20/√100 = 2.
Question 10
For the population in Question 9 (n = 100), P(x̄ < 77) is:
  • a) 0.4404
  • b) 0.9332
  • c) 0.0668
  • d) 0.4332
  • e) None of the above
z = (77 − 80)/2 = −1.5. P(Z < −1.5) = 1 − 0.9332 = 0.0668. (a) forgets the √n.
Question 11
A sample of 20 delivery times (normal population) has x̄ = 38.4 min and s = 6.2 min. A 95% confidence interval for μ is:
  • a) (35.498 ; 41.302)
  • b) (35.683 ; 41.117)
  • c) (36.003 ; 40.797)
  • d) (25.423 ; 51.377)
  • e) None of the above
σ unknown, n < 30 ⇒ t19; 0.975 = 2.093. 38.4 ± 2.093 × 6.2/√20 = 38.4 ± 2.902. (b) wrongly uses 1.96; (c) uses t19;0.95.
Question 12
The critical value used for a 98% confidence interval for μ with σ unknown and n = 10 is:
  • a) 2.326
  • b) 2.262
  • c) 2.764
  • d) 2.821
  • e) None of the above
df = 9, column 1 − α/2 = 1 − 0.01 = 0.99 ⇒ t9; 0.99 = 2.821.
Question 13
In a survey, 132 of 400 shoppers used a mobile app. A 90% confidence interval for the population proportion is:
  • a) (0.284 ; 0.376)
  • b) (0.291 ; 0.369)
  • c) (0.306 ; 0.354)
  • d) (0.269 ; 0.391)
  • e) None of the above
p̂ = 0.33. 0.33 ± 1.645√(0.33 × 0.67/400) = 0.33 ± 0.039. (a) uses 1.96.
Question 14
Using p̂ = 0.33 from Question 13, how many shoppers are needed to estimate p to within 0.03 with 95% confidence?
  • a) 665
  • b) 943
  • c) 944
  • d) 1068
  • e) None of the above
n = 0.33 × 0.67 × (1.96/0.03)² = 943.75 ⇒ round UP to 944. (1068 is the p = 0.5 answer.)
Question 15
A Type II error is committed when:
  • a) H₀ is rejected when it is true
  • b) H₀ is not rejected when it is false
  • c) H₁ is rejected when it is false
  • d) the significance level is too small
  • e) None of the above
Type II = failing to reject a false H₀ (probability β).
Question 16
A manager claims that a new process has reduced the mean assembly time from 14 minutes. The hypotheses are:
  • a) H₀: μ = 14; H₁: μ < 14
  • b) H₀: μ = 14; H₁: μ > 14
  • c) H₀: μ = 14; H₁: μ ≠ 14
  • d) H₀: x̄ = 14; H₁: x̄ < 14
  • e) None of the above
"Reduced" ⇒ left-tailed, about μ (not x̄).

For questions 17 and 18: a political party claims 60% support. In a random sample of 200 voters, 108 support the party. Test whether support differs from 60% at α = 0.05.

Question 17
The test statistic is:
  • a) −1.703
  • b) −0.060
  • c) −1.732
  • d) 1.732
  • e) None of the above
p̂ = 108/200 = 0.54. z = (0.54 − 0.60)/√(0.6 × 0.4/200) = −0.06/0.03464 = −1.732. (a) wrongly uses p̂ under the root.
Question 18
The correct decision is:
  • a) Reject H₀, since −1.732 < −1.645
  • b) Reject H₀, since |−1.732| > 1.645
  • c) Do not reject H₀, since −1.732 < 1.96
  • d) Do not reject H₀, since |−1.732| < 1.96
  • e) None of the above
"Differs" ⇒ two-tailed ⇒ z0.975 = 1.96. Compare the absolute value: 1.732 < 1.96 ⇒ do not reject. (a)/(b) use the one-tailed value — a very common slip.

For questions 19 and 20: the standard deviation of fill weights must not exceed 3 g. A sample of 25 packets (normal population) gives s = 4 g. Test at α = 0.05 whether the variance exceeds the limit.

Question 19
The test statistic is:
  • a) 32.000
  • b) 42.667
  • c) 44.444
  • d) 18.000
  • e) None of the above
χ² = (n − 1)s²/σ₀² = 24 × 16/9 = 42.667. (a) forgets to square both; (c) uses n instead of n − 1.
Question 20
The critical value and decision are:
  • a) 36.42; reject H₀
  • b) 39.36; reject H₀
  • c) 13.85; do not reject H₀
  • d) 36.42; do not reject H₀
  • e) None of the above
Right-tailed, df = 24, column 0.95 ⇒ 36.42. 42.667 > 36.42 ⇒ reject H₀: the variance exceeds the limit.

Mock BPart 1 — Long Questions [30]

Question 1 [7]

Flaws in a roll of fabric occur randomly at an average rate of 2.4 flaws per 10 metres.

(i) State two properties of a Poisson experiment that this situation satisfies. [2]
(ii) What is the probability that a 10-metre length has no flaws? [1]
(iii) What is the probability that a 10-metre length has at least three flaws? [2]
(iv) What is the probability that a 25-metre length has exactly five flaws? [2]

Memo — Question 1

(i) Any two ✓✓: flaws are counted in an interval (length of fabric); the number of flaws in one length is independent of the number in another non-overlapping length; the probability of a flaw is the same for equal lengths and proportional to the length; two flaws at exactly the same point is practically impossible (rare events).

X ~ Poisson(2.4). (ii) P(X = 0) = e−2.4 = 0.091 ✓
(iii) P(X ≥ 3) = 1 − [P(0) + P(1) + P(2)] = 1 − (0.0907 + 0.2177 + 0.2613) ✓ = 0.430 ✓
(iv) Y ~ Poisson(2.4 × 2.5 = 6) ✓. P(Y = 5) = e−66⁵/5! = 0.161 ✓

Question 2 [8]

Marks in a large first-year module are normally distributed with mean 62 and standard deviation 9.

(i) What percentage of students score above 75? [2]
(ii) What is the probability that a randomly chosen student scores between 55 and 70? [2]
(iii) The top 20% of students are invited to a tutoring programme. What is the minimum mark needed? [2]
(iv) For a random sample of 36 students, what is the probability that their mean mark is below 60? [2]

Memo — Question 2
(i) P(X > 75) = P(Z > 1.44) ✓ = 1 − 0.9251 = 0.0749 ⇒ 7.49% ✓
(ii) P(55 < X < 70) = P(−0.78 < Z < 0.89) ✓ = 0.8133 − (1 − 0.7823) = 0.596 ✓
(iii) P(X > k) = 0.20 ⇒ P(Z < z) = 0.80 ⇒ z = 0.84 ✓ ⇒ k = 62 + 0.84(9) = 69.56 ✓
(iv) σx̄ = 9/√36 = 1.5. P(X̄ < 60) = P(Z < −1.33) ✓ = 1 − 0.9082 = 0.092 ✓

Question 3 [7]

The time (in minutes) that customers wait at a bank is normally distributed.

(i) From long experience the population standard deviation is 2.5 minutes. A sample of 40 customers had a mean waiting time of 18.6 minutes. Construct a 98% confidence interval for the mean waiting time. [2]
(ii) A separate sample of 15 customers had a standard deviation of 3.2 minutes. Construct a 95% confidence interval for the population standard deviation of waiting times. [3]
(iii) How many customers must be sampled to estimate the mean waiting time to within 0.5 minutes with 95% confidence (σ = 2.5)? [2]

Memo — Question 3
(i) σ known ⇒ z. 18.6 ± z0.99(2.5/√40) = 18.6 ± 2.326(0.3953) ✓ ⇒ (17.681 ; 19.519) ✓
(ii) df = 14: χ²14; 0.975 = 26.12, χ²14; 0.025 = 5.63 ✓
CI for σ²: (14 × 3.2²/26.12 ; 14 × 3.2²/5.63) = (5.489 ; 25.464) ✓
CI for σ: (√5.489 ; √25.464) = (2.343 ; 5.046) minutes ✓
(iii) n = (1.96 × 2.5/0.5)² = 96.04 ✓ ⇒ 97 customers ✓ (round up)

Question 4 [8]

A call centre trains its agents on a new system and records the average call-handling time (minutes) of eight agents before and after the training:

Agent12345678
Before8275906877857180
After7874846972807075

Assuming the differences are normally distributed, test at the 5% level of significance whether the training reduced the mean call-handling time.

Memo — Question 4
1. H₀: μbefore = μafter    H₁: μbefore > μafter ✓✓  (paired — same agents)
2. α = 0.05 ✓
3. d = before − after: 4, 1, 6, −1, 5, 5, 1, 5   (Σd = 26, Σd² = 130)
    d̄ = 3.25, sd = √[(130 − 26²/8)/7] = 2.550 ✓
    tts = 3.25/(2.550/√8) = 3.606 ✓
4. tcrit = t7; 0.95 = 1.895 ✓
5. Reject H₀ if tts > 1.895. Since 3.606 > 1.895, reject H₀ ✓
6. At the 5% level of significance there is sufficient evidence to conclude that the training reduced the mean call-handling time ✓
Equivalent: d = after − before (values −4, −1, …), H₁: μafter < μbefore, t = −3.606 < −1.895 ⇒ reject. Either is right as long as the order of the differences matches the order in H₁ (the lecturer stresses this in the Ex 7A/7B videos).

Which situation is best modelled by a binomial distribution?

  • b) The time a customer waits in a queue

Question 2
X ~ BI(15, 0.2). P(X = 0) is:
  • a) 0.035
  • b) 0.000
  • c) 0.200
  • d) 0.132
  • e) None of the above

Question 3
For X ~ BI(15, 0.2), the mean and standard deviation are:
  • a) 3 and 2.4
  • b) 3 and 3
  • c) 0.2 and 0.16
  • d) 3 and 1.549
  • e) None of the above

  • b) 0.45

P(2) + P(3) + P(4) = 0.20 + 0.15 + 0.10 = 0.45 (or 1 − 0.30 − 0.25).
Question 5

  • a) 13 and 4
  • b) 15 and 6
  • c) 13 and 18
  • d) 13 and 16
  • e) None of the above
E = 3(5) − 2 = 13. V = 3²(2) = 18 (the −2 disappears).
Question 6
Customers arrive at 0.5 per minute (Poisson). The probability that no customer arrives in a 4-minute period is:
  • a) 0.607
  • b) 0.135
  • c) 0.018
  • d) 0.500
  • e) None of the above
λ = 0.5 × 4 = 2 ⇒ P(0) = e−2 = 0.1353. (a) uses λ = 0.5.
Question 7
X ~ N(30, 16). P(26 < X < 34) is:
  • a) 0.6826
  • b) 0.9544
  • c) 0.8413
  • d) 0.1974
  • e) None of the above
σ = 4 ⇒ P(−1 < Z < 1) = 0.8413 − 0.1587 = 0.6826. (b) is what you get if you take σ = 2 instead of √16 = 4 — always square-root the variance.
Question 8
The value z such that P(Z > z) = 0.10 is:
  • a) −1.28
  • b) 0.13
  • c) 1.645
  • d) 1.28
  • e) None of the above
P(Z < z) = 0.90 ⇒ closest 0.8997 ⇒ z = 1.28.
Question 9
A machine fills bags with mass ~ N(250 g, 4² g²). The proportion of bags heavier than 258 g is:
  • a) 0.9772
  • b) 0.4772
  • c) 0.0228
  • d) 0.0013
  • e) None of the above
z = (258 − 250)/4 = 2 ⇒ 1 − 0.9772 = 0.0228.
Question 10
For the machine in Question 9, four bags are sampled. P(x̄ > 253) is:
  • a) 0.2266
  • b) 0.0668
  • c) 0.9332
  • d) 0.0228
  • e) None of the above
σx̄ = 4/√4 = 2 ⇒ z = 1.5 ⇒ 1 − 0.9332 = 0.0668. (a) uses σ = 4 (one bag).
Question 11
25% of a town's households have solar panels. In a sample of 300 households, P(p̂ < 0.20) is:
  • a) 0.9772
  • b) 0.4772
  • c) 0.1587
  • d) 0.0228
  • e) None of the above
σp̂ = √(0.25 × 0.75/300) = 0.025. z = (0.20 − 0.25)/0.025 = −2 ⇒ 0.0228.
Question 12
Holding everything else constant, increasing the confidence level from 90% to 99% will:
  • a) widen the interval
  • b) narrow the interval
  • c) leave the width unchanged
  • d) shift the centre of the interval
  • e) None of the above
A larger critical value (1.645 → 2.576) means a larger margin of error. The centre stays at x̄.
Question 13
n = 9, x̄ = 12, s = 3 (normal population). A 90% CI for μ is:
  • a) (10.355 ; 13.645)
  • b) (10.140 ; 13.860)
  • c) (10.040 ; 13.960)
  • d) (9.694 ; 14.306)
  • e) None of the above
t8; 0.95 = 1.860. 12 ± 1.860 × 3/3 = 12 ± 1.86. (a) uses z = 1.645; (d) uses t8;0.975 (95%).
Question 14
The t-distribution compared with the standard normal distribution:
  • a) is skewed to the right
  • b) has thinner tails
  • c) has heavier tails, and approaches the normal as df increases
  • d) can take only positive values
  • e) None of the above
Symmetric about 0, heavier tails; with df = ∞ it is the standard normal. (d) describes χ².
Question 15
To estimate μ to within 5 units with 99% confidence when σ = 20, the minimum sample size is:
  • a) 62
  • b) 87
  • c) 106
  • d) 107
  • e) None of the above
n = (2.576 × 20/5)² = 106.17 ⇒ 107. (62 uses 1.96; 106 rounds down.)
Question 16
An analyst wants to know whether two independent machines produce bolts with different mean lengths. H₁ is:
  • a) μ₁ ≠ μ₂
  • b) μ₁ > μ₂
  • c) μ₁ < μ₂
  • d) x̄₁ ≠ x̄₂
  • e) None of the above
"Different" ⇒ two-tailed, in terms of population means.

For questions 17 and 18: H₀: μ = 500 vs H₁: μ ≠ 500. σ = 20, n = 64, x̄ = 495, α = 0.05.

Question 17
The test statistic is:
  • a) −0.250
  • b) −2.000
  • c) −16.000
  • d) 2.000
  • e) None of the above
z = (495 − 500)/(20/8) = −5/2.5 = −2.
Question 18
The decision is:
  • a) Do not reject H₀ since −2 < 1.96
  • b) Do not reject H₀ since |−2| < 2.576
  • c) Reject H₀ since |−2| > 1.96
  • d) Reject H₀ since −2 < −1.645
  • e) None of the above
Two-tailed at 5% ⇒ ±1.96. |−2| = 2 > 1.96 ⇒ reject H₀.
Question 19
A paired t test is carried out on 10 matched pairs. The degrees of freedom are:
  • a) 20
  • b) 18
  • c) 10
  • d) 9
  • e) None of the above
The pairs collapse into one sample of 10 differences ⇒ df = n − 1 = 9.
Question 20
Two independent samples of 10 each (normal, equal variances): x̄₁ = 24, s₁ = 4; x̄₂ = 21, s₂ = 5. The pooled t test statistic is:
  • a) 1.482
  • b) 0.663
  • c) 3.000
  • d) 1.500
  • e) None of the above
sp = √[(9 × 16 + 9 × 25)/18] = √20.5 = 4.528. t = 3/(4.528 √(1/10 + 1/10)) = 3/2.025 = 1.482.
